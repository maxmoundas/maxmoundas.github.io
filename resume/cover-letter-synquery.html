Dear Synquery Team,

What you're building at Synquery sits at an intersection I've spent two years navigating — getting AI to work reliably with the kind of knowledge that lives in people's heads, not in documents. At Vanderbilt's Amplify Generative AI Innovation Center, I've built enterprise AI platforms, agentic systems, and knowledge-capture workflows for thousands of users across higher education. When I came across Synquery's mission to use AI agents to find and interview domain experts — and turn that unwritten expertise into usable, shareable knowledge — I recognized both the problem and the ambition. I'd like to be part of the team solving it.

On the systems side, I know what it takes to build AI infrastructure that scales. Amplify serves 18,000+ users at Vanderbilt and is actively deployed at universities across the country, running on AWS with serverless architecture and infrastructure-as-code built to stay cost-efficient as usage grows. Multi-tenant deployment across institutions meant solving real problems around data isolation, per-client configuration, and integration with each school's existing tools — Office 365, authentication providers, APIs. Every university came with different constraints, and the system had to flex around them while staying maintainable. That kind of work sharpens your instincts for where to build for scale from day one and where to stay lean until you need it.

Understanding what a client actually needs — versus what they initially ask for — is something I've developed deliberately. Through the Amplify Staff Fellows program, I worked with staff from departments across Vanderbilt who each came with a problem they believed AI could solve, but with no shared technical vocabulary and very different existing workflows. My job was to understand their systems, scope what was realistic, and build something that fit into how they actually worked rather than asking them to adapt around the technology. That process — listening, reframing, building, iterating — is close to what an agent-driven expert interview workflow demands, and it's where I do some of my best work.

I move fast and learn by building things that have to work. When I joined Amplify as one of three engineers, generative AI was barely an enterprise concept. Since then I've shipped a production multi-university platform, published four peer-reviewed papers on LLMs and prompt engineering, built agentic desktop applications, and taught a week-long AI course to doctoral candidates across Europe — all while the underlying models, APIs, and best practices changed around us. Early-stage teams need people who can pick up new tools and domains quickly and apply them without slowing down, and that's how I've operated throughout my career.

I'm in Nashville and genuinely excited about what Synquery is building. I'd love to talk about where I could be most useful — in person or otherwise, whenever works for you.
